CircuitSavvy

Bringing aerospace engineering to students.

CircuitSavvy is a student-led program in Delaware. Students learn how space payloads work, come up with their own experiments, and write full proposals for the NASA TechRise Student Challenge.

02Process

Four phases, from a question to a payload.

  1. Phase 01

    Learn

    How payloads behave in space, what the atmosphere does to them, and everything else you have to account for when you design for flight.

  2. Phase 02

    Brainstorm

    Students bring their own ideas. We work on them together until they hold up as real research questions.

  3. Phase 03

    Draft

    Each student writes a proposal for the NASA TechRise Student Challenge: a full design description, schematics, and hardware specifics.

  4. Phase 04

    Build & fly

    If a proposal wins a TechRise grant, students build the payload and their experiment is launched to space.

03Ideas

Questions students brought to the whiteboard.

  1. Can stabilizers protect cargo better during launch?
  2. How does microgravity change the way metals react to propulsion and gravity?
  3. Can expanding foam shield against debris in space?
  4. Do self-healing polymers still work in space?
  5. How does radiation affect spacecraft materials?
  6. Could a floating electromagnet system make landings seamless?
  7. Do soft metals and gels keep their chemical bonds when frozen in space?
